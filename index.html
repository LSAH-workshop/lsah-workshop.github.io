A shared research question

What should the model learn?
What should the harness do?

An agent’s policy emerges from a learned model and the program around it: context, tools, memory, search, verification, and control flow. We bring together post-training, reinforcement learning, program synthesis, and agent systems to study how these pieces work together and adapt across sessions as environments evolve—and where to invest computation.

01 / Research directions

Six ways into
the same question.

When do learning and harness design substitute for one another? When do they become more powerful together? We welcome methods, theory, systems, controlled studies, and negative results.

01

Post-training for
downstream harnesses

How should objectives, data, and curricula prepare models for the tools, memory, search, and verification they will use? Study training–deployment mismatch and when post-training amplifies or reduces the gains from external mechanisms.

Objectives & curricula   /   Harness families   /   Deployment mismatch

02

Learning to generate
and improve harnesses

Can models learn to write and revise the programs that control their own execution? Explore program synthesis, search, and feedback-driven improvement of workflows, tools, memory, and verifiers.

Program synthesis   /   Reliability   /   Generalization

03

Harness-aware inference
scaling laws

How do post-training, harness design, and inference compute jointly determine performance? Develop predictive scaling relationships with explicit accounting for tokens, tool calls, latency, and harness development.

Compute allocation   /   Cost accounting   /   Held-out predictions

04

Joint learning and
co-adaptation

How can model parameters and harness decisions be optimized jointly or alternately? Study joint reinforcement learning, co-evolving skills and policies, credit assignment, and which capabilities should be distilled into weights.

Joint optimization   /   Credit assignment   /   Distillation

05

Evaluation and attribution
of model–harness systems

Separate gains from model learning, harness changes, their interaction, and additional resources. Use component swaps, crossed evaluations, matched budgets, and failure analyses that account for tuning and stochastic variability.

Controlled comparisons   /   Benchmark quality   /   Failure analysis

06

Continual learning and
personalization through harnesses

How can long-running agents learn and personalize across sessions as environments evolve? Learn memory and retrieval policies, reusable skills, and workflows from histories and user feedback. Study adaptation in weights versus persistent harness state and code, retention versus stale behavior, and long-term performance, transfer, and cumulative adaptation cost.

Persistent memory   /   User feedback   /   Long-term adaptation
